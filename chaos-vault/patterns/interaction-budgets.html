Interaction Budgets

User-owned contracts that determine how much sequencing, choice, depth, information, and commitment an interface may ask for during one bounded task.

5 Production-derived1 Prototype-derived
Core Position
Declared load, not detected emotion

The person chooses what would help. The model may structure a task, but it does not choose the adaptation, estimate emotional severity, or silently reduce access. Every mode is previewed, approved, explainable, reversible, temporary, and stable for its bounded scope.

CV-IBG-001

Declared Load, Not Detected Emotion

Ask what the interface should demand less of instead of inferring whether the person is anxious, depressed, overwhelmed, distracted, or “low capacity.” The adaptation follows an explicit request, not a covert classification.

InputsOne step at a time, fewer decisions, protect progress, defer optional work.
GuaranteesNo camera, sentiment score, behavior-based capacity rating, or permanent profile.
RequiredPreview, explicit approval, why-this-view explanation, immediate exit.
CV-IBG-002

Interaction Budget

An engineering primitive that converts user-declared needs into deterministic presentation policies. The budget belongs to the task, not the person, and expires with the task context.

Policy examplesSequence steps, cap visible choices, preserve drafts, mark optional work, suppress decorative motion.
Model boundaryThe model may propose task structure. It may not infer or choose the budget.
PersistenceSeparate temporary storage, visible scope, explicit clear, bounded expiry.
CV-IBG-003

Minimum Necessary Interface

The smallest complete set of information, decisions, and actions required to finish one user-selected task. “Minimum” refers to interaction demand, not truth, access, or completeness.

ContainsGoal, completion condition, required steps, real decisions, verified facts, review.
Always availableFull plan, edit, cancel, copy, download, and exit.
NeverGenerated HTML, hidden consequences, automatic submission, or unsupported deadlines.
CV-IBG-004

Tone-as-Consent Dial

Negotiate emotional temperature before the system asks for reflection, vulnerability, conflict, or effort. Flame.io’s strongest idea was not relationship scoring. It was letting people choose playful, romantic, deep, or chaotic before the prompt arrived.

Portable formsPractical / gentle / direct. Just record / help me understand / go deeper.
ControlsChange tone, skip, stay shallow, return later.
QuarantineAI grading of vulnerability, creativity bonuses, relationship-quality scores, streak punishment.
CV-IBG-005

Three Valid Endings

A completed artifact remains complete whether the person keeps it, releases it, or carries one obligation forward. The productive ending is not treated as the morally superior ending.

KeepConfirmed archive write. The artifact remains frozen and intact.
ReleaseExact local deletion after confirmation with a bounded undo window.
Carry forwardCreate a separate temporary task context. Do not mutate the completed artifact.
CV-IBG-006

Stable Temporary Mode

Once a person approves a changed mode, keep the interface predictable for the bounded task. Continuous affective reconfiguration can create more workload than it removes.

StableLayout, step order, control location, disclosure rules, and mode label.
TemporaryTask-scoped storage, expiration, explicit clear, no permanent identity claim.
ExitFull view and end-mode controls remain consistently reachable.
